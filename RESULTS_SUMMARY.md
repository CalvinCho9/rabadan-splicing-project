## Cohort

935 BRCA tumor samples, 106 matched normal samples, HLA-A, both allele slots
per sample, 39 distinct HLA-A alleles observed (top 20 cover 95% of
allele-carrying samples — `hla_a_02_01`, `hla_a_03_01`, `hla_a_01_01` are the
three most common). 122/935 (13.0%) tumor samples and 19/106 (17.9%) normal
samples carry at least one alternative-splicing (AS) event by our filter
(novel junction, >10% of local reads, downstream of an annotated junction).
Most carriers have exactly one event; nobody has more than two.

## Finding 1 — The pipeline generalizes past BRCA without code changes

**Takeaway:** the analysis was built schema-first (group by `[Type, Barcode,
Allele]`, thresholds as CLI flags, no BRCA- or HLA-A-specific logic baked in),
so it already runs unmodified on other cohorts and is ready for HLA-B/C the
moment that data lands.

**Number:** pointed `analyze_brca.py`'s loading/bucketing/classification
functions at `data/pan_HLA-A_SJ_combined.tsv` (a separate, already-available
multi-cohort file spanning NORMAL + TCGA-CHOL/HNSC/KIRC/LGG/UCEC — not BRCA)
with zero code edits: loaded 9,738 rows across 301 samples and 6 cohorts,
found 46 AS events, correctly bucketed and classified by the same
`Annotated_cumsum` / geometry logic. This is a capability check, not a new
biological result — it confirms the pipeline is reusable, it doesn't claim
anything about those cohorts' splicing.

**Shows it:** no dedicated figure (a code-generalization check, run directly
against `data/pan_HLA-A_SJ_combined.tsv`); the CLI flags that make it
possible (`--local-rate-threshold`, `--exon-skip-min-jump`,
`--intron-retention-max-shift`, `--min-allele-samples`, `--min-carriers`,
`--exclude-alleles`) are documented in `analyze_brca.py`'s module docstring.

## Finding 2 — Testing the paper's two events in BRCA: an underpowered null, quantified

**Takeaway:** at this sample size, neither exon5_skip nor intron5_retention
shows a statistically confident tumor-vs-normal difference — and we can now
say precisely *how* underpowered we are, not just "not significant."

**Numbers** (A\*11:01 excluded — see Finding 3):

| Event | Tumor | Normal | Fisher p | Odds ratio (95% CI) | Achieved power¹ | MDE at 80% power² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exon5_skip | 0.1% (1/935, CI 0.0–0.6%) | 0.0% (0/106, CI 0.0–3.5%) | 1.0 | 0.34 (0.01–8.45)³ | 9.8% | tumor rate would need to be outside [0.0%, 2.0%] |
| intron5_retention | 4.2% (39/935, CI 3.1–5.7%) | 7.5% (8/106, CI 3.9–14.2%) | 0.13 | 0.53 (0.24–1.17) | 29.4% | tumor rate would need to be outside [1.8%, 16.8%] |

¹ power to detect the *observed* effect at α=0.05, two-sided (two-proportion
z-test, Cohen's h). ² minimum detectable effect at 80% power given the actual
n=935/106 — i.e. the range we'd have needed to fall outside of to call this
significant. ³ Haldane-Anscombe corrected (a cell was 0).

exon5_skip is essentially unobserved (one tumor case) — achieved power 9.8%,
nowhere near enough data to say anything. intron5_retention trends
*depleted* in tumor — the **opposite** of the paper's predicted enrichment —
but at only 29.4% achieved power, and with an 80%-power detection floor of
±several points around the 7.5% normal baseline, this cohort could not have
reliably detected a real effect of the size the paper reports either
direction. **This is a power problem, not a disagreement with the paper.**

A one-at-a-time sweep of all three judgment-call thresholds (local-rate
cutoff 5–20%, exon-skip jump 50–150bp, intron-retention shift 20–70bp; 12
settings total) keeps the direction "depleted in tumor" in **all 12** and
OR between 0.41–0.60 throughout — the conclusion doesn't hinge on having
picked one specific cutoff. (One setting, local-rate=15%, crosses p<0.05;
treat that as expected noise around a boundary given 12 tests, not a
separate finding — none of this survives multiple-testing correction below.)

## Finding 3 — HLA-A\*11:01's "event" is a constitutive isoform, not tumor biology

**Takeaway:** what looked like a strikingly recurrent intron5_retention
signal was actually a property of one allele's reference transcript, present
in essentially every carrier regardless of tumor/normal status. It has been
diagnosed and excluded from the headline/per-allele comparisons above (it's
the reason Finding 2's intron5_retention numbers dropped from 12.8%/17.9% to
4.2%/7.5% once removed).

**Numbers:** 118/122 (96.7%) A\*11:01-carrying tumor samples and 19/19 (100%)
A\*11:01-carrying normal samples show the same junction
(`2365_2806->2383_2806`) — no other allele in the cohort comes close to this
penetrance, and the rate is identical in tumor and normal. Read-count check:
the "novel" junction carries only 27.4% of local reads on average (tumor
27.5%, normal 26.3%) — the *annotated* junction stays dominant at 72.4% in
both groups — so this isn't a simple reference-coordinates-swapped artifact;
it looks like a real, fixed, ~25–30%-penetrant minor splice isoform baked
into A\*11:01's reference transcript, unrelated to cancer status. In a
tumor-only pass (no matched normals to compare against), this allele's high
recurrence rate alone would have looked like a promising cancer-associated
signal — having normals is what caught it.

**Shows it:** `outputs/fig_per_allele.pdf`, `figures_overview.pdf` panel B ·
diagnostic detail and per-allele penetrance table in
`outputs/annotation_artifact_check.csv` and
`outputs/annotation_artifact_raw_rows_hla_a_11_01.csv` · full writeup in
`ANNOTATION_ARTIFACT_NOTE.md`.

## Finding 4 — HLA-C tumor-vs-normal in BRCA: also an underpowered null, same honest treatment

**Takeaway:** HLA-C's classifier was validated for use as-is (Phase 1 —
naive/unflipped geometry produces coherent, plausible splicing patterns;
the strand-aware flip was tested and empirically rejected, see
`outputs/PHASE1_BC_strand_check.md`). Its BRCA tumor-vs-normal comparison
looks like HLA-A's: directionally suggestive, statistically inconclusive.

**Numbers** (933 tumor, 106 normal; no alleles excluded — no C allele showed
an A\*11:01-style constitutive pattern):

| Event | Tumor | Normal | Fisher p | Odds ratio (95% CI) | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| exon5_skip | 0.1% (1/933, CI 0.0–0.6%) | 0.9% (1/106, CI 0.2–5.2%) | 0.194 | 0.11 (0.01–1.81) | depleted in tumor |
| intron5_retention | 3.1% (29/933, CI 2.2–4.4%) | 0.9% (1/106, CI 0.2–5.2%) | 0.354 | 3.37 (0.45–24.98) | enriched in tumor |

Both odds-ratio CIs cross 1 — **neither result is statistically confident**,
same rule as HLA-A. Worth flagging for the room: intron5_retention's
*direction* here (enriched in tumor) matches what the MHC Hammer paper
predicts, unlike HLA-A's BRCA result (which trended the opposite way) — but
this rests on a single normal-side carrier (1/106), so the CI is enormous
(0.45–25×) and this is not a replication, just a directionally-consistent,
very fragile point estimate. 0 of 4 tested per-pattern comparisons in the
detailed table survive BH-FDR q<0.05.

**Shows it:** `outputs/fig_classes_HLA-C.pdf`, `figures_overview.pdf` panel
A2 · `outputs/table_headline_classes_HLA-C.csv` ·
`outputs/results_BRCA_tumor_vs_normal_HLA-C.csv` (per-pattern + FDR) ·
`outputs/fig_volcano_HLA-C.pdf` / `figures_overview.pdf` panel C (combined
with HLA-A) · `outputs/fig_per_allele_HLA-C.pdf`.

## Finding 5 — HLA-A\*11:01's constitutive isoform generalizes beyond BRCA (pan-cancer, descriptive)

**Takeaway:** the BRCA-established constitutive junction for A\*11:01
(`2365_2806->2383_2806`, Finding 3) recurs at similarly high penetrance in
other cancer types and in an independent normal-tissue pool — strengthening
the case that this is a fixed, allele-intrinsic minor isoform rather than a
BRCA-cohort quirk. **Descriptive only** — most pan-cancer cohorts here are
tumor-only, and the separate `NORMAL` pool is not matched-normal to any one
cancer type, so this is cross-cohort *recurrence*, not a new
tumor-vs-normal test.

**Numbers:** of the 4 pan-cancer cohorts with any A\*11:01 carriers at all
(TCGA-KIRC and TCGA-UCEC had none to test), 3 show near-universal
penetrance of the BRCA pattern — NORMAL pool 20/20 (100%), TCGA-CHOL 3/3
(100%), TCGA-LGG 6/7 (85.7%) — while TCGA-HNSC is an outlier at 1/3 (33.3%,
too small an n to weigh against the other three). Pooled across all
pan-cancer cohorts: 30/33 (91%) of A\*11:01 carriers show the pattern.

A broader scan (every allele with ≥10 pan-cancer samples, its own top
recurrent pattern, penetrance by cohort) turned up no *other* constitutive
allele for HLA-A — the only (allele, cohort) combinations at ≥80%
penetrance were A\*11:01 itself, in the same three cohorts above. The
equivalent scan for HLA-C found **no** cross-cohort constitutive artifact
(max penetrance 50%, on n=2 — noise); HLA-C's own real-variable-splicing
allele from Finding 4 (`hla_c_03_04`) shows modest, cohort-variable
penetrance (0–21%) wherever it's observed, consistent with genuine variable
splicing rather than an artifact.

**Shows it:** `outputs/PHASE3_pancancer_descriptive.md`,
`outputs/table_phase3_A11_01_cross_cohort.csv`, `figures_overview.pdf`
panel D · full per-allele/per-cohort tables:
`outputs/table_phase3_allele_penetrance_by_cohort_HLA-{A,C}.csv`.

## Bottom line

BRCA alone neither confirms nor refutes the MHC Hammer HLA-A/C exon5_skip /
intron5_retention findings — both cohorts are underpowered on the normal
side (106 samples; achieved power 10–29% for HLA-A's two target events, and
HLA-C's numbers are similarly wide) for effects this size, and for A that's
a quantified statement, not a hand-wave. What this pass *did* deliver: a
reusable, schema-driven pipeline verified against a second dataset and now
extended to two more genes, statistically honest (CI + power + FDR-aware)
tests of the two target events for both A and C that hold up under
threshold sensitivity (A) and exclude no real signal via mislabeling (C), a
descriptive picture of both cohorts' allele diversity, and two concrete
data-quality catches — A\*11:01's constitutive isoform (now shown to
generalize pan-cancer, Finding 5) and, this round, the discovery that
HLA-B's classifier cannot yet be trusted at all (Finding 3 for B is simply
"insufficient data," not a null result) — each caught specifically *because*
the pipeline was pointed at held-out data (matched normals for A/C, other
cancer types and normal tissue for A\*11:01, a strand/geometry validation
step for B/C) rather than trusted on its first output.
